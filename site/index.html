My ad script briefs

Here's what's inside the brief I wrote for RYZE.

  • Every line gets its own row: the image, what happens on screen and the exact line. Three hooks, then the body and the CTA. Your editor sees the whole ad before cutting a single frame.

  • Every person gets a character sheet from all sides, so they look the same in every shot.

  • Every place gets a set sheet and a seating chart, so rooms, doors and seats stay the same from shot to shot.

  • Every image in the script sits in one folder, numbered and named by hook and row. Your editor just goes down the list.

  • One table on top: the concept, who it's for, the format, where the ad sends people, the hypothesis, and what we do if it wins or loses.

  • Clear rules for your editor: a color legend, timing, captions, how to say the brand name and how the product looks.

  • The primary text, headline and button text, ready to paste into Ads Manager.

  • Every claim in the ad has a source: the brand's own pages and its winning ads in the Meta Ad Library.

The first things I do when I come on board

  1. I watch your winning ads

    I break them down by format, hook, awareness stage and more. Then I look for patterns.

  2. I fill the gaps

    If I see a gap, I create an ad that fills it, with a hypothesis.

  3. I brief your editors

    I write the scripts and briefs your video editors need.

  4. I test and build on what wins

    I check NC CPA, CTR and hook rate, then build the next round on the winners.
